## recalc_stat_to_p

<!-- description:recalc_stat_to_p -->
Recompute a p-value from ONE printed test statistic: t, chi2, z, r or F. Use it wherever a statistic and its p sit in a TABLE or in prose statcheck does not parse -- statcheck verifies these same five distributions, but only as a text scanner over APA grammar, so the same numbers in a table cell were invisible to the whole kit. `recalc_f_to_p` was the only value-level sibling. MEASURED: three archived papers hit this, and on one it downgraded the HEADLINE finding from `impossible` to `highly_suspicious` -- the review wrote "No tool in this kit converts a chi-squared statistic plus df to a p-value". IF THE PAPER DOES NOT PRINT THE DF OF A t OR chi2, CALL IT ANYWAY AND OMIT `df` -- never guess one. For a fixed statistic a t's p FALLS as df grows and a chi-squared's p RISES, so the tool bounds p over EVERY df in [1, infinity) and a reported p outside that interval is unreachable at every df the paper could have used: `chi2 = 6.0, p = .001` fails at every df (the smallest attainable p is about .014, at df = 1), while `t = 2.0, p = .06` with no df stays clean because some df reconciles it. MEASURED 2026-10-18: two of the tools agent's five misses on the p-value-errors working set were statistics printed without df that this tool then refused (a retracted paper's `t = 9.748, p = .523`: p is at most .065, at df = 1, so no df reaches .523). `df_min`/`df_max` narrow the range ONLY when the paper prints numerals that force it -- a stated N caps a t's df at N - 1; a design you inferred does not (rulebook rule 4) -- and the result's `verdict_needs_df_bounds` says whether the verdict rested on them. An F needs both df (its p is not monotone in them), an r needs n; without them this refuses and NAMES what is missing. ROUNDED STATISTIC, so it implies a p RANGE: `chi2 = 4.1` asserts [4.05, 4.15) and the reported p is judged against the whole achievable interval. Capped at `suspicious`: an OVER-claim (a p below what the statistic supports) is flagged under every relation; an UNDER-claim only when the p was printed exact. A one-tailed test or a correction would explain a disagreement, so this never accuses. `tails` is ignored for chi2 and F, whose p is an upper tail by construction -- but AT df = 1 that upper-tail p IS the two-tailed p of the underlying t or z (F(1,k) = t(k)^2, chi2(1) = z^2), so half of it is the one-tailed p and a directional hypothesis explains a reported p that is half what you compute. DO NOT write that a one-tailed reading is impossible for a 1-df F or chi2; it is not, and doing so produced a false accusation. BUT THAT READING COUNTS ONLY WHEN THE PAPER STATES IT (statcheck's rule): pass `one_tailed_stated=True` when the paper says, anywhere, that its tests are one-tailed, one-sided or directional (quote it), and a p reachable only one-tailed is then `indeterminate` -- for a two-tailed t, z or r too. Otherwise the mismatch is `suspicious` with failure_mode `reconciles_only_one_tailed_unstated` and the one-tailed reading named beside it. MEASURED 2026-10-18: the unstated rescue hid 21 of the 49 errors in Wicherts, Bakker & Molenaar's (2011) hand coding and decided no correct statistic. Parameters: statistic (float), kind (str), df, df2 (a fractional df -- Welch, Greenhouse-Geisser -- is accepted and its own rounding counted), n, reported_p, decimals_stat, decimals_p, tails, p_relation, p_adjustment, one_tailed_stated, df_min, df_max (only with df omitted). SAY HOW THE P WAS PRINTED: pass `p_relation` '=' for an exact p ('p = .03'), '<' for a bound ('p < .05'), '>' for 'p > .05'. An EXACT p is checked in BOTH directions: one printed LARGER than anything the inputs allow is an under-claim, returned `suspicious` (never higher) with failure_mode `reported_p_above_recomputed`, and `decision_error` true when it crosses .05. MEASURED: 't(38) = 3.94, p = .696' (true p .00034, a documented decision error) came back `consistent` before this. A bound is checked only against over-claiming, since 'p < .05' is true of any smaller p -- and a DECLARED bound is written exactly, with no rounding slack: 'p < .01' against a true .0149 is an over-claim (MEASURED 2026-10-18 on the 1,120 statistics Wicherts, Bakker & Molenaar 2011 coded by hand, this slack hid most of the missed bound errors). Left unset, a value that is no conventional bound (.696, .26) is read as exact and .05/.01/.001 keep the bound reading -- so an exact 'p = .05' or 'p = .001' needs '=' said out loud. PASS `p_adjustment='sphericity'` (Greenhouse-Geisser / Huynh-Feldt with the uncorrected df printed) or `'multiplicity'` (Bonferroni, Holm, Tukey, Scheffe: an adjusted p beside its raw statistic) for each statistic a correction the paper DECLARES covers -- the prompt quotes the sentence when it finds one. A corrected p is larger than its statistic implies, so that direction then returns `indeterminate` (failure_mode `under_claim_explained_by_adjustment`); a printed p SMALLER than the statistic allows is never explained by a correction. MEASURED: all 12 false flags on the 1,071 hand-coded correct Wicherts statistics were Huynh-Feldt p's. PASS `sampling='complex_survey'` WHEN THE PAPER SAYS SO -- survey or sampling weights, a complex survey design, NHANES WTMEC*/SDMVPSU, R's survey package, Stata svy:, PROC SURVEY*. This check then returns `indeterminate` instead of running, because a design-based estimate and its standard error are not functions of the printed means, SDs or counts: weighting moves the composition of the sample, not just its variance, so a disagreement is expected in BOTH directions and means nothing. MEASURED on an NHANES paper: six of one run's twenty severity-bearing tool findings were this one error.
