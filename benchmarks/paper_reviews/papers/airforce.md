Multivitamin Compliance Reduces Injuries of Female Recruits at Air Force Basic Training: A Randomized Controlled Cohort Study 

 

This is a forensic review of the following paper:

https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article/189/Supplement_3/93/7735884

This study administers an enhanced educational program about the benefits of multivitamin supplementation to Air Force recruits. They report this information reduces musculoskeletal injuries over basic training.

The study has a central problematic feature that makes recalculation impossible.

 



The first five results are impossible to recalculate because nine of the ten initial numbers stated fail GRIM.

The only number consistent with the cell size is 100%, but this presents an additional problem: there is no mention of how a chi-squared test was calculated (and valid) with a cell size of zero. Potentially the authors would want to use Fisher's Exact Test, but this is not mentioned.

It is also uncertain why there are three putative results for the single-item comparisons from one single survey, not a 2*3 chi-squared test run on the results of the whole survey. It should also be noted at this point that the percentage sum of the compliance survey data is more than 100% for n=79 and less than 100% for n=80.

This data is extremely confusing and incoherent, and in my opinion the results should not be trusted without the raw data being available for re-analysis.
